# Phantom Architecture

A catalog of twenty-five designs that never left the page.

The buildings in this catalog share no common style, no era, and no scale. What they share is a single strange property: they were drawn, modeled, staked out, and sometimes begun — but they were never built. Some were stopped by money, some by politics, some by war, some by physics. A few were overruled by the architect's own death. Most were simply too large an idea for the world that held them.

They are phantoms. They exist in plates, in sketches, in wooden models in museum basements. The drawing survives; the building never began.

The project is inspired by Philip Wilkinson's *Phantom Architecture* (2017). The cases are the well-known ones — Boullée's sphere, Tatlin's spiral, Wright's mile, Hadid's opera house — the canonical set that any such catalog must cover. The prose and the illustrations are new.

## A note on illustrations

Every entry carries a schematic elevation rendered to SVG in a unified blueprint style. These are not photographic reconstructions. They are silhouettes — the essential form, a ground line, a 1.8-metre human figure for scale. Where the original drawing is precise (Boullée), the SVG approximates it; where the original is a collage or a mood-piece (Archigram, Ferriss), the SVG imposes more structure than the source strictly permits.

The generator is deterministic and lives in `scripts/generate_illustrations.py`. Running it produces identical output each time, which is the minimum a catalog owes its reader.

See [Method & sources](about.md) for the approach and the shared bibliography.
